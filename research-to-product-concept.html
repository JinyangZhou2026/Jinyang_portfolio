01 / Context & Design Question

Designing a slower reward loop through tangible play.

Context & Audience

Children aged 6–10, playing at home or in shared learning environments.

The project asks whether a slower, hands-on activity can make progress legible without relying on continuous digital stimulation.

Design Question

How might tangible play create a clear sequence of construction, prediction, correction, and reward?

02 / Final Product & Interaction

A physical sequence becomes a melody—and a reason to continue the story.

Interaction Logic

The reward arrives after the child builds, predicts, and tests.

The proposed story module supports the sequence; the physical construction remains the primary interaction.

  1. 01

    Choose a story

    Insert a narrative card and listen until the story pauses.

  2. 02

    Read the sequence

    The module displays the musical pattern to recreate.

  3. 03

    Build the path

    Configure tracks and place color-coded musical keys.

  4. 04

    Release the ball

    The ball strikes the keys and plays the sequence.

  5. 05

    Continue or retry

    A correct melody advances the story; errors invite adjustment.

03 / Simulation, Prototype & Validation

Digital and physical tests separated simulated motion from real-world contact.

SolidWorks Motion Study

Checked ball travel and sequential key contact.

Used before prototyping to review path continuity and possible interference.

3D-Printed Bench Test

Revealed contact and friction the simulation could not represent.

Observed ball release, key contact, and stall points under assembly tolerances.

Observation → Design Response

Contact consistency & key support

Visible stalls at stepped transitions and quieter early keys pointed to smoother transitions, stiffer keys, elastic supports, and a resonance cavity.

Next Test

Acoustics, electronics & use

Still required: acoustic tuning, note recognition, durability, and child comprehension and replay testing.

04 / Research to Design Criteria

Observed behavior became four product rules.

Interview / Self-report

Relaxing feedback could also be difficult to leave.

One interview associated screen use with relaxation, difficulty stopping, and shorter sustained attention.

Domestic Observation

Self-directed sequencing created a quieter focus.

During one after-school session, restlessness eased once the child began arranging objects into a repeated sequence.

Shared-activity Observation

Clear shared structure helped the group stay with the task.

A shared goal supported attention and negotiation; added complexity disrupted the sequence.

  1. 01 / ChallengePredictable steps with adjustable difficulty
  2. 02 / FocusConstruction before digital feedback
  3. 03 / RewardMelody and story mark visible progress
  4. 04 / RetryReconfigurable modules make errors recoverable

Scope: a small qualitative study used to guide concept development—not to claim behavioral or clinical outcomes.

Decision 02 / Architecture & Form

Which architecture best supports that interaction?

Selected System Direction

Compact tabletop panel and task-focused module

Selected because The physical path remains primary, while the smaller module clearly supports story, score, and the next action.

Development EvidenceReview three architecture alternatives and identified risks
Proposal 01

Separate story reader

Risk identified It split the information hierarchy and left system feasibility unresolved.

Proposal 02

Tall easel architecture

Risk identified Its footprint and elevated interaction area conflicted with seated tabletop play.

Proposal 03

Large narrative module

Risk identified Its visual volume competed with the child-built physical path.

06 / Product System & CMF

One modular platform supports construction, music, storytelling, and calm domestic use.

System Architecture

Configurable parts make difficulty and melody visible.

Track height adjusts challenge; key color and length encode notes; the narrative module handles story, score, and the next action.

  • Foldable perforated play panel
  • Reconfigurable stepped tracks
  • Color-coded musical keys
  • Detachable narrative module and story cards

Narrative Module

Story, score & audio verification

A proposed NFC workflow compares the played sequence with a stored score; the module can detach for listening.

Electronics not yet engineered

Physical Platform

Fold, configure & correct

A perforation matrix, modular tracks, collection tray, and folding support simplify setup, correction, and storage.

Preliminary construction proposal

Anthropometric Reference / Ages 7–10

A preliminary play envelope

Reference data limited the active area to 310 × 462.5 mm and track width to 91 mm for seated reach and grip.

Fit for six-year-old users still requires testing

CMF Material Evidence

Real samples informed a calm, functional palette.

Warm, lightly textured surfaces reduce visual noise, while vivid keys preserve note identification.

  • Light woodWarm domestic base and visible grain
  • Textured polymerSofter touch and lower visual glare
  • Anodized metalClear functional color cues for the notes

Sample-level evaluation—production materials and finishes remain proposed.
