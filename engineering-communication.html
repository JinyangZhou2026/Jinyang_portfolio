02 / My Role & Workflow

Five decisions behind every approved visualization.

  1. 01

    CAD Preparation & Privacy

    In SolidWorks I add visible liquid or gas volumes, rebuild missing presentation details, remove non-operational hooks or service parts, and simplify confidential geometry.

    Decision Show function without exposing unnecessary engineering detail.

  2. 02

    CMF & Camera

    I compare render materials with physical samples and manufactured finishes, then choose the viewpoint that makes interfaces and hierarchy easiest to read.

    Decision Accuracy and legibility before visual polish.

  3. 03

    Flow & Timing

    I structure stroke, valve timing, and flow direction from engineer briefings, factory observation, and protected sectional drawings.

    Decision Turn operating logic into a readable sequence.

  4. 04

    Three Review Gates

    I validate CMF, review critical keyframes, and approve a low-resolution video preview before committing to batch rendering.

    Decision Correct expensive errors before final output.

  5. 05

    Post-production & Delivery

    I render approved sequences in batches, then edit them in Premiere Pro and After Effects with branding, explanatory text, and required visual effects.

    Decision Adapt one verified technical story to each channel.

04 / Technical Deep Dive — 3130N Plunger Pump

Connecting product architecture, mechanical motion, and fluid behavior.

The 3130N combines a power end, three reciprocating plunger assemblies, valve timing, and two fluid phases—relationships that an exterior view cannot explain. I structured one sequence that moves from product construction to operating principle without losing the engineering logic.

  • Product architecture
  • Mechanical motion
  • Valve & flow timing
  • Customer film
Power end & product envelope retained Liquid-end interfaces preserved

01 / Engineering Input

Reduce CAD complexity without breaking the system.

  • Engineering baseline

    The SolidWorks input established the product envelope, interfaces, service features, and presentation geometry.

  • My preparation

    I removed non-operational details, rebuilt missing presentation elements, and controlled disclosure depth before developing the internal sequence.

What could change Presentation detail, removable parts, and disclosure depth.
What could not change Axes, interfaces, component order, and mechanical continuity.

Crank rotation becomes the starting cue Three motion paths stay comparable

02 / Communication Decision

Reveal the mechanism before explaining the flow.

  • Sequence structure

    Separate the major assemblies, reveal the crank and plunger paths, then move into the liquid-end section.

  • Communication logic

    Give viewers a spatial model first so valve and flow changes retain a clear mechanical cause.

Design principle Establish where motion comes from before asking the viewer to interpret hydraulic timing.

03 / Engineering Verification

Check motion, valve state, and flow as one timeline.

  • Engineer review

    Check detail visibility, fluid representation, and valve timing in relation to plunger motion.

  • Validation output

    I exported keyframes and a low-resolution preview to resolve issues before batch rendering.

  1. Gate 01Geometry & CMFDetail visibility, material identity, and disclosure level
  2. Gate 02Critical keyframesValve state, plunger position, and corresponding fluid direction
  3. Gate 03Preview approvalFluid color, timing, camera sequence, and explanatory rhythm
