02 / Context & Challenge

Research was converted into visual criteria—not presented as brand history for its own sake.

Brand Context

Sound, design, and craft form one premium experience.

Bang & Olufsen positions its products through acoustic performance, iconic Danish design, and uncompromising craftsmanship. For this study, those pillars became working criteria for motion, light, composition, and material treatment.

Official brand context ↗
Product Context

Beoplay H95 / Premium over-ear headphones

The H95 combines adaptive active noise cancellation, adjustable ANC and transparency controls, soft leather, memory-foam cushions, and a foldable architecture designed for listening on the move.

Official product page ↗
Experience Context

Luxury is communicated through control and detail.

Physical dials make volume and noise control tactile; leather and memory foam support passive isolation and comfort. These attributes offered stronger visual storytelling opportunities than a list of specifications.

Official control guide ↗

02B / Consumer Communication

Premium headphones contain complex technology, but consumers do not purchase specifications alone.

Technical Truth

Adaptive ANC, physical controls, foldable mechanics, and layered materials.

These product attributes are meaningful, but a feature list alone cannot communicate how the product should feel to own and use.

Consumer Truth

People need to imagine focus, comfort, control, and pride of ownership.

The visualization therefore had to connect engineering and material evidence with an emotional listening experience.

Design Question

How might product visualization make sound, comfort, material quality, and control emotionally understandable before the product is experienced in person?

Concept AudienceDesign-conscious music listener
Purchase MotivationImmersion without sacrificing material quality
Launch ContextProduct page, social motion, and retail display

04 / Creative Direction

A quiet visual system designed to make precision feel desirable.

Creative Territory

Controlled Immersion

The direction balances dark graphite structure with warm Golden Tone highlights. Cameras orbit slowly, reflections reveal rather than decorate, and transformation is used only where it clarifies a product experience.

  • Precise, not clinical
  • Luxurious, not ornamental
  • Immersive, not visually noisy
Color & Material Logic
Golden Tone Warm Porcelain Graphite Deep Black
Camera Language

Orbit, pause, inspect.

Full-product silhouettes establish recognition; controlled close-ups then reveal tactility, controls, and construction.

Motion Language

Measured transformation.

Rotation and folding communicate function. Slower material moments create contrast and give the product visual weight.

AI-Assisted Workflow / Transparent Use

Reconstructing a missing supporting design-sheet asset.

The original CGI render was used as a visual reference to generate a new supporting sketch sheet for this case study. AI supported asset reconstruction and ideation; the product CGI, scene composition, materials, lighting, animation planning, and final edit remain presented as authored visualization work.

05 / Storyboard & Motion Architecture

The film was planned as a product story—not a collection of unrelated effects.

Sequence Logic
  1. Attract

    Establish silhouette and a premium visual tone.

  2. Reveal

    Introduce the full product and Golden Tone finish.

  3. Explain

    Use movement to clarify controls and foldable structure.

  4. Desire

    Slow down for material, lining, and comfort details.

  5. Close

    Return the product to a composed ownership scene.

Storyboard → Final Motion

From a planned product reveal to the rendered camera movement.

06B / Reflection, Contribution & Credits

Success is assessed through communication criteria—not invented campaign impact.

Product Recognition

Can the viewer identify the product and finish quickly?

The final silhouette, logo area, and Golden Tone surface should remain legible across hero and social formats.

Feature Legibility

Does movement clarify function rather than distract?

Rotations and transformations should connect directly to control, portability, material, or comfort.

Brand Consistency

Do camera, color, and timing support the same premium proposition?

The system should feel quiet, precise, tactile, and coherent across every touchpoint.

Next Validation

Test understanding with target consumers.

The next iteration would compare message recall, refine sound design, and produce lighter channel-specific exports.

Personal Contribution

Art direction, scene direction, look development, animation planning, rendering, editing, and portfolio presentation.
